# D1.1 — Claim and evidence policy

## Claim-level evaluation

Evaluate individual claims, not an author, school of thought, website, or paper as a whole. One source may support a narrow mechanism while not supporting a broader health claim.

## Keep these dimensions separate

- Framework: which interpretive lens the claim belongs to.
- Theoretical status: how established or provisional the model is.
- Evidence type: the design and source of the evidence.
- Risk of bias: limitations in the evidence.
- Certainty: confidence in the specific claim across relevant evidence.
- Applicability: match to the participant and the tracker's measurement method.
- Clinical actionability: whether the evidence supports a clinical action.

Do not collapse these into a weighted score or rank bioenergetic and mainstream sources on a single scale.

## Bioenergetic-first does not mean certainty-first

The bioenergetic framework is the project's primary interpretive lens. It should be explored first and presented prominently. That is a project design choice, not a claim that Ray Peat's or Ling's hypotheses have been clinically validated.

For each bioenergetic claim:
1. Identify the original source and exact proposition.
2. Separate the author's claim from later community interpretations.
3. Label the proposed mechanism and theoretical status.
4. Identify the type of supporting evidence.
5. Search for relevant contradictory or null evidence.
6. State whether human clinical outcome evidence exists.
7. Do not use the hypothesis alone to recommend treatment.

## Mainstream clinical comparison

Clinical guidelines, diagnostic criteria, systematic reviews, and human trials belong in a distinct comparison section. Explain agreement, disagreement, and differences in scope. Do not reduce this section to a generic disclaimer.

## Safety override

Safety information is mandatory and can override the primary framework. Never advise stopping prescribed medication, delaying urgent assessment, or replacing standard care with an unvalidated theory. Supplement and medication records must include dose/context limits, known interaction concerns, and a clinician/pharmacist review note where relevant.

## Citation integrity

Store DOI, PMID, guideline identifier, or stable URL where available. Record publication date and verification date. If a citation cannot be checked, mark the record as unverified/draft and do not present its claim as established. Never fabricate missing bibliographic details.

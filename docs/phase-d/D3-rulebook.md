# D3 — Rulebook and interpretation boundaries

## Rule 1: observation before interpretation

Always state the data before explaining it. Example: "Across 8 recorded mornings from 3–14 November, temperature median was X °F; 2 mornings had no pulse entry." Do not begin with a label such as "slow metabolism".

## Rule 2: compare like with like

Compare the same checkpoint, temperature method, units, and similar timing. If method or checkpoint differs, split the series or mark it non-comparable.

## Rule 3: within-person change is not a diagnosis

A change from the person's own baseline may be worth reviewing, but it does not establish pathology or a mechanism.

## Rule 4: bioenergetic-first presentation

The first interpretive section is bioenergetic. It must be populated only by eligible knowledge records. The model's theoretical status and evidence limitations must be visible in the same section.

## Rule 5: physiology is a separate layer

Mechanistic physiology must not be used as a shortcut to clinical efficacy. Label human, animal, cellular, and theoretical evidence accurately.

## Rule 6: mainstream comparison is explicit

Present relevant clinical guidelines and human evidence in their own section. Explain differences in population, endpoints, and measurement context.

## Rule 7: counterevidence is mandatory

For each non-trivial interpretation, provide known counterevidence or state that counterevidence has not yet been reviewed. Absence of a counterevidence record is not proof of consensus.

## Rule 8: safety can override

When a verified safety rule applies, show it before speculative interpretation and advise appropriate professional assessment. No preferred framework can overrule urgent safety.

## Rule 9: no false precision

No composite health score, metabolic score, thyroid score, disease probability, or single evidence score. Completion percentages are not health status.

## Rule 10: user notes are observations, not verified facts

Quote or summarize participant-entered text as "reported". Never transform it into a diagnosis or confirmed exposure.

## Rule 11: no automatic intervention

The engine may suggest better measurement consistency or questions to discuss with a clinician. It may not prescribe a diet, supplement, drug, or dose.

## Rule 12: every claim is traceable

Every generated interpretation must cite record IDs and source details. Unsupported claims are omitted, not generated from model memory.

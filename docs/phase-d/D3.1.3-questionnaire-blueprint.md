# D3.1.3 — Revised questionnaire blueprint

## Design principle

The current form is an intake questionnaire, not a validated diagnostic instrument. Redesign it as a structured history and observation capture system. Its job is to collect interpretable observations, not manufacture a score.

## Proposed user flow

1. **Core profile** — age, relevant context, current diagnoses, medication list, recent illness, consent.
2. **Current priorities** — user-defined concerns, onset, course, impact, and what they want to understand.
3. **Repeated measurements** — temperature, pulse, weight, waist, sleep, and selected performance observations.
4. **Symptoms and function** — discrete symptom items with onset, frequency, severity description, duration, and functional impact.
5. **Nutrition and meal response** — representative food records and repeatable reaction events.
6. **Sleep, light and environment** — actual daily events plus habitual patterns.
7. **Training and recovery** — exposure, protocol-defined tests, injuries and recovery.
8. **Optional sensitive modules** — sexual/reproductive questions with explicit skip controls.
9. **Goals and practical constraints** — resources, barriers, tracking capacity, and next step.

## Global wording rules

- State the recall window in every question: today, past 7 days, past 30 days, or usual pattern over a defined period.
- Ask one construct per question. Split compound prompts.
- Use neutral wording; avoid leading terms such as “metabolic slowdown” or “hormonal imbalance”.
- Add “not sure”, “not measured”, “not applicable”, and “prefer not to answer” only where semantically appropriate.
- Keep “none” distinct from unanswered.
- For frequency, use explicit time anchors and mutually exclusive bins.
- For intensity, define anchors in plain language; do not assume equal spacing.
- Do not mix a diagnosis, symptom, suspected cause, and family history in one response field.
- Do not force estimates. “Unknown” is a valid response.

## Priority redesign backlog

**P0 — integrity and safety**
- consent and sensitivity controls;
- explicit missingness states;
- diagnosis/symptom/suspicion separation;
- medication and supplement repeatable records;
- source synchronization for all IDs, labels and options;
- preserve unknown keys and legacy answers.

**P1 — high-value repeated observations**
- temperature and pulse records with method/site and timestamp;
- weight and anthropometry with provenance;
- sleep events and actual schedule;
- symptom event capture;
- food-reaction events;
- daily food records.

**P2 — improve estimates**
- quantities, units, cooked/dry basis, confidence and source;
- caffeine/alcohol timing and quantities;
- training volume and test protocols;
- light exposure timing and duration.

**P3 — context**
- environment, work schedule, social support and goals;
- keep these descriptive and outside diagnostic inference.

## Proposed structured fields

### Symptom event
`id`, `symptom_code`, `description`, `started_at`, `ended_at`, `frequency`, `intensity_descriptor`, `functional_impact`, `context`, `suspected_triggers`, `provenance`, `confidence`.

### Food event
`date_time`, `food`, `quantity`, `unit`, `preparation`, `cooked_or_dry`, `brand_or_source`, `meal_context`, `symptoms_after`, `onset_latency`, `reproducibility`, `confidence`.

### Medication/supplement event
`name`, `product`, `dose_value`, `dose_unit`, `route`, `frequency`, `start_date`, `stop_date`, `indication`, `prescriber`, `status`, `observed_effects`, `adverse_effects`. Do not infer safety or efficacy from adherence.

### Sleep event
`date`, `bed_time`, `estimated_sleep_onset`, `wake_events`, `final_wake_time`, `out_of_bed_time`, `nap_events`, `estimated_duration`, `confidence`, `context`.

### Measurement
`id`, `type`, `value`, `unit`, `recorded_at`, `site`, `device`, `protocol`, `state`, `provenance`, `confidence`, `context`.

## Do not build

- a single “metabolic score” from temperature, pulse, libido, mood, and digestion;
- an unvalidated 1–5 sum of subjective answers;
- disease probabilities from questionnaire responses;
- automated supplement recommendations;
- automatic causal conclusions from before/after observations;
- a ranking of people based on agency, mood, body composition, or sexual responses.

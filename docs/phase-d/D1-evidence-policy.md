# Evidence policy for the Ray Peat Health Tracker

## Claim-level evidence

The unit of evaluation is the claim, not the author, website, or paper as a whole. One paper may support one narrow claim and fail to support a broader claim.

## Required distinctions

- Evidence type is descriptive, not a guarantee of quality.
- Risk of bias describes systematic limitations.
- Certainty describes confidence in a specific claim across the relevant body of evidence.
- Applicability describes whether the population and measurement match the participant.
- Mechanistic plausibility is not clinical efficacy.
- Association is not causation.
- A reference interval is not automatically a treatment threshold.
- A group average is not an individual's target.

## Bioenergetic material

Ray Peat-inspired claims and Ling's association-induction hypothesis may be included as historical or theoretical models. Each record must:
- identify the original source where possible;
- state the proposed mechanism as a hypothesis;
- distinguish primary source statements from later interpretations;
- include contemporary supporting and contradictory evidence;
- state whether relevant human outcome evidence exists;
- avoid using the theory alone to justify a clinical action.

## Safety

The knowledge layer must not recommend stopping or changing prescribed medication, replacing clinical care, or taking high-dose supplements. Drug/supplement interaction records should direct the reviewer to authoritative references and clinician/pharmacist review. Emergency symptoms and urgent-care thresholds require current, jurisdiction-appropriate clinical sources.

## Citation integrity

Store DOI, PMID, guideline identifier, or stable URL when available. Record publication date and last-reviewed date. If a source cannot be verified, do not publish the claim as an evidence-backed record.
